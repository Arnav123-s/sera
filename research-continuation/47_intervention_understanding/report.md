## What was learned and how it is used

Passive decay alone leaves several explanations: irreversible phase loss, static phase spreading, or a combination. Their passive curves depend on the same sum of rates. SERA retains that equivalence class. After a controlled pulse, the candidate models predict different readout probabilities. Independent shot outcomes train nonnegative numerical coefficients on the existing R1 owner; separate evidence selects and qualifies their use.

The learner selects the installed pulse-loss candidate when independent selection evidence improves enough, then checks its adequacy separately. It also receives an independent log of which requested pulses actually happened. This prevents an unsuccessful command from masquerading as evidence against the intended mechanism. An unread performed measurement and an omitted measurement have distinct event semantics through the existing R2 instrument.

The original question is preserved throughout: predict a plus-X readout after a specified 2.5-second history and investigate which explanation accounts for passive decay and the intervention. The returned answer includes assumptions, alternatives, model confidence and adequacy separately. Its practical interfaces support conditional prediction, finite control comparison and an evidence-ranked explanation.
