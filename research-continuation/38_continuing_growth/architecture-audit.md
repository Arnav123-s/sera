I extend `AcquiredR1` as `GrowingR1`. The new `growth_policy` is a registered computational parameter head for procedure selection. A checkpoint contains the inherited owner, exact updated weights, procedure optimizer, RNG, action history, high-water scores and original goal.

The retained memory supplies source and request representations. Cached book/reading features are source-hash checked. Request features are computed by the actual current recurrent encoder. Its parameters remain fixed in this continuation; the words, human annotations, allowed procedures and mechanical task family are supplied resources. SERA learns the continued readout weights and procedure preferences from practice outcomes. Cross-strand effects are measured separately from common ownership.

The loop observes independent development errors, proposes a practice action, executes a trial update, checks every strand, accepts or rejects the result and credits verified progress. Trial rejection retains the event and cost. Repeated progress cannot generate a fresh high-water bonus. Event identities bind the action, source manifest, original goal and before/after knowledge. Restoring a checkpoint replays the credit ledger and checks ordering and stale knowledge. Sealed final labels do not enter action selection, gradient updates or reward.

Previously supplied mechanics programs remain conditional alternatives with explicit assumptions. Their practice labels come from the retained independent screen. These examples do not become observed physical facts, new scientific laws or source-quality qualifications. Source admission, empirical-vs-exact distinctions and novel-definition gates remain inherited. Old signed portfolio qualifications remain associated with their original owner; a changed owner does not silently inherit those signatures.

Admission requires a prospectively selected checkpoint to pass its locked final gate. Procedure admission separately crosses old/new knowledge with old/new procedure on future groups. Each admission is scoped to its measured task families.
