# Focused primary-source research

I used two targeted literature searches: finite-family risk calibration and reusable program abstraction. Each returned five search slots, including duplicate versions of the same works. This is not ten independent papers or a new comprehensive literature review. The earlier 25-work review remains historical evidence.

1. [Learn then Test: Calibrating Predictive Algorithms to Achieve Risk Control](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.01052v5). I inspected the introduction and the finite-family testing framework, including its family-wise error control statement. The useful distinction is between choosing a policy under controlled selection and trusting an uncalibrated score. GG-GUARD-002 uses a fixed finite policy family and independent-world binomial bounds; it does not claim distribution-free control under an arbitrary mechanism change.
2. [DreamCoder: Growing Generalizable, Interpretable Knowledge with Wake-Sleep Bayesian Program Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08381). I inspected its abstract and the start of the paper concerning learned reusable abstractions and search. GC-001/002 implement a much smaller trace-abstraction test with supplied formal roles and algebra rules. They do not reproduce DreamCoder or train a neural proposal distribution.
3. [Microsoft: Job object memory limits](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winnt/ns-winnt-jobobject_basic_limit_information) and [extended limit/accounting fields](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winnt/ns-winnt-jobobject_extended_limit_information). These specify the job-wide committed-memory limit and failed allocations on attempted excess. The native test observed rejection of a 512 MiB allocation under a 256 MiB job limit; it also exposed a higher reported high-water count. I preserve that observation instead of treating the peak field as resident memory or a proof that every transient counter stayed below the cap.

The supplied v3 synthetic calibration and alias studies are supporting packet evidence, not measurements of the new SERA component. Their strict cross-runtime replay failures remain disclosed. The compatibility diagnosis changes only explicitly bounded numeric comparisons; no learning is rerun and no archived source or result is overwritten.

The next primary uncertainty is positive transfer from acquired executable knowledge into shared learning. A lower execution count for a compiled finite program and a shared identity do not resolve that question.
