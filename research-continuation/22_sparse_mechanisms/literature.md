# What I took from the primary research

I read Tao's own explanations, the Candès–Romberg–Tao stability paper, the original Sparse MRI paper and the modified-CS support-reuse paper. The dates below are publication dates, not claims that these techniques were invented in this project. My experiments adapt established algorithms to explicit SERA questions.

| Primary source | Reading and engineering consequence |
|---|---|
| [Terence Tao, *Compressed sensing and single-pixel cameras* (2007)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2007/04/13/compressed-sensing-and-single-pixel-cameras/) | Tao describes measurement signatures as “a linear algebra analogue of a hash function.” Unknown important coefficients, a suitable representation and distinguishable measurements matter together. I compared pixel and Haar representations, dense controls, regular aliases and random sampling. I counted reconstruction and encoding costs separately. The analogy is mathematical; it is not a cryptographic integrity guarantee. |
| [Tao, *Compressed Sensing* (2008)](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2008/02/06/compressed-sensing/) | His main post explicitly raises incorporating prior support and choosing adaptive measurements as questions; it does not claim that every adaptive policy helps. I tested acquired support against a wrong prior, and disagreement queries against random and space-filling choices under observation and work budgets. Reader comments on that page are not attributed to Tao. |
| [Tao, *Compressed sensing — Or: the equation Ax=b, revisited*, slides](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2009/08/compressed-sensing1.pdf) | Pages 12–13 distinguish uniqueness from tractable recovery. Pages 19–21 discuss stronger matrix conditions and approximate/noisy recovery. Page 23 presents the dual coding problem: expand a message and recover sparse corruption. These motivate separate compression and error-repair studies. Pages 7 and 17 present simplified Fourier-imaging examples; they do not establish clinical reconstruction quality. |
| [Candès, Romberg and Tao, *Stable Signal Recovery from Incomplete and Inaccurate Measurements* (2005 revision)](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0503066v2) | Theorems 1 and 2 require restricted-isometry assumptions and a bounded observation error. Approximate sparsity adds a tail term. A small residual alone is not their theorem. I report empirical column diagnostics without calling them an RIP certificate, and separate unknown omitted mechanisms from bounded sensor noise. The experiments' infinity-norm LP constraint is an engineering variant, not a literal numerical reproduction of the paper's Euclidean-noise program. |
| [Lustig, Donoho and Pauly, *Sparse MRI* (2007), author PDF](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~mlustig/CS/SparseMRI.pdf) | MRI acquires Fourier information; useful sparsity may be in a transform or image gradients. Sampling geometry and variable density interact with that representation. Practical Cartesian acquisitions differ from unconstrained random points. The paper also examines low-contrast losses. I implemented an ideal Fourier model with matched point/line sample counts and a faint-feature diagnostic. My separable Haar/pixel penalty omits the paper's combined wavelet/total-variation method, multiple coils and scanner constraints; this is a synthetic analogue, not a reproduction or medical validation. |
| [Vaswani and Lu, *Modified-CS: Modifying Compressive Sensing for Problems with Partially Known Support*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.5066) | Partially known support can change the recovery objective, but incorrect support can hurt. I leave previously inferred support unpenalized, record the cost of learning it from an earlier situation, and compare a deliberately wrong support. It is knowledge reuse under a supplied dictionary, not discovery of an improved learning procedure. |
| [Bora et al., *Compressed Sensing using Generative Models* (2017)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/bora17a.html) | Restricting recovery to a generator's range is another structural prior. A supplied or pretrained generator is a substantive assumption, with range mismatch a possible failure. This release does not quietly replace a sparse dictionary with an unrestricted learned generator. |

## The crossword analogy, made precise

The useful intuition is that many possible outputs can be determined by a small number of underlying choices. However, clues work only when they distinguish the allowed possibilities. With a known dictionary, write `y = A c + e`, where `c` is the active coefficient vector. If two allowed coefficient vectors give the same measurements, no decoder can know which one was intended from those measurements alone.

For an s-sparse vector, independence of every set of 2s columns of A suffices for uniqueness: subtract two candidate solutions; their difference has at most 2s nonzero coordinates and would otherwise lie in the nullspace. Efficient L1 recovery needs stronger conditions. Two or three observations can therefore be enough for a very restricted problem; that is not a rule for arbitrary puzzles, memories or neural networks. [Tao's uniqueness argument and recovery discussion](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2009/08/compressed-sensing1.pdf).

My finite-precision check is simple counting: if a memory can take N distinct admissible values, an exact representation needs at least `ceil(log2(N))` bits. Restricting the admissible family reduces N; the decoder cannot remove that requirement. For s nonzero entries chosen among p positions and q nonzero coefficient values per entry, the family has `choose(p,s) * q^s` elements. This is why I report seed, dictionary, representation, precision and storage costs rather than implying unlimited memory.

## Five different objectives

- **Mechanism recovery:** minimize the coefficient L1 norm while fitting independently observed values within a declared noise bound. Test predictions outside the observed coordinates and allow rejection.
- **Memory sketch:** store `A x`; an incoming update `d` changes the stored state by `A d`. This supports linear updates, but storing known sparse entries directly can be smaller and exact.
- **Sparse error repair:** store redundant measurements `b = A x + e` with more measurements than payload coordinates; minimize the corruption norm while leaving x unpenalized. Extra storage pays for fault tolerance.
- **Weight adaptation:** keep a feature map fixed and fit a sparse delta in `y - f_parent(x) = Phi(x) delta`. If many weights must change, or the missing behavior is outside the feature map, the premise fails.
- **Imaging:** minimize Fourier data error plus a pixel/Haar sparsity penalty. A well-optimized wrong prior can still produce a wrong image. This is visible in the regular-alias and dense-texture controls.

These are classical numerical mechanisms. Neither quantum hardware nor Schrödinger/Dirac dynamics were required for the measured effects. They contribute candidate operators to the source architecture; they do not replace its ownership, evidence, validity or learning-to-learn contracts.
