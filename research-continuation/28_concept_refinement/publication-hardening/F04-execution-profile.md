# F04: qualify the saved owner's numerical execution profile

The constraint diagnostic on Linux matched the exact corrected owner. Its four score differences were at most 1.64e-11, and its learned search starting coordinates differed by 5.96e-8 (one float32 unit at that scale). The selected roles, mode, physical model and remaining random starting points matched. Other remote workers rejected the earlier exact factual-buffer replay. The raw failures and diagnostic records are retained; they were not relabeled as passing.

The local reference machine is an Intel Core i7-10870H. Its recorded execution profile is Torch AVX2 and NumPy X86_V3, with one numerical thread. The workflow now caps Torch and MKL at AVX2, uses the Haswell OpenBLAS kernel, disables NumPy X86_V4 dispatch and sets the same one-thread environment as local work. The controlled local replay under these explicit settings is byte-identical to the unmodified local replay log, including zero interpretation and constraint differences. The parent factual/constraint source and all their exact checks remain unchanged.

This is a reproducibility profile, not a claim that matching version strings guarantees matching arithmetic. Every CI worker still has to reconstruct the exact owner and validate its saved branches. The final publication receipt records whether each operating system passed that qualification. The measured Linux discrepancy is preserved in `linux-constraint-diagnostic.json`; `pre-portability-source/constraint-runtime.py` retains the audited unchanged strict validator.

The settings follow the published [PyTorch CPU-dispatch implementation](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/v2.10.0/aten/src/ATen/native/DispatchStub.cpp), [OpenBLAS runtime controls](https://www.openmathlib.org/OpenBLAS/docs/runtime_variables/), [NumPy SIMD configuration](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/global_state.html) and [MKL instruction-set dispatch](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/docs/onemkl/developer-guide-windows/2023-1/instruction-set-specific-dispatch-on-intel-archs.html).

The constraint diagnostic's first local version restored its older parent rather than the later corrected parent and failed the owner-identity assertion. That source and failed job are preserved. The corrected diagnostic restores the actual parent's observations and weights while holding its branch records aside solely for an independent comparison; it never saves or admits the temporary diagnostic session.
