# Mechanism choices

I use a finite sparse dictionary as the initial quantitative acquisition mechanism. Brunton, Proctor and Kutz describe identifying compact governing relationships by regression over candidate functions in [their original SINDy paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1517384113). Here the setting is different: named textbook quantities, dimensions and worked supervision are supplied; the selected parameters become persistent executable knowledge.

Dimensional restrictions provide a useful comparison with unrestricted fitting: few examples can fit many algebraically distinct functions. The source-equation certificate supplies a separate semantic admission condition. Exact source-equation execution is the strong installed-rule control; nearest-example reuse measures what can be recovered by remembering a calculation alone.

The source authority is the original OpenStax University Physics publisher repository, commit `2300d5b8ca2571dacc6ec25ebef56ba8d49000a2`. The supervised transcription, variable naming, sparse dictionary, checkers and acquisition policy are engineering contributions.

I preserve quantity, change, rate and relationship as explicit distinctions. Momentum, impulse and force have related roles: momentum is a state quantity, impulse is its change under an applied force over time, and force is its rate of change. The executable task must state which quantity is intended and preserve direction, mass and interval assumptions.
